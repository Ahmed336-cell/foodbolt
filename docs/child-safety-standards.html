FoodRush — Child Safety Standards

Application name (Google Play): FoodRush

Developer: FoodRush

Last updated: 28 August 2026

These publicly published standards apply to FoodRush, the group food-ordering app on Google Play. They comply with Google Play’s Child Endangerment and Child Safety Standards policy.

Zero tolerance: FoodRush has a zero-tolerance policy for Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM). Users may not create, upload, or distribute content that facilitates the exploitation or abuse of children.

1. Published prohibition of CSAE

FoodRush explicitly prohibits users from using the app to endanger children. This includes, but is not limited to:

  • Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) in any form
  • Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) — creating, uploading, requesting, sharing, or distributing
  • Inappropriate interaction targeted at a child
  • Child grooming — befriending a child online to facilitate sexual contact or exchange sexual imagery
  • Sexualization of a minor — content that depicts, encourages, or promotes sexual abuse or exploitation of children
  • Sextortion — threatening or blackmailing a child using real or alleged access to intimate images
  • Trafficking of a child — advertising or soliciting a child for commercial sexual exploitation
  • Using FoodRush rooms, invite links, display names, orders, notes, or receipt photos for any of the above

FoodRush is not directed at children under 13. Users must meet the minimum age required in their country. FoodRush rooms are invite-only and not a public social feed.

2. In-app mechanism for reports and feedback

FoodRush provides in-app ways for users to submit concerns and reports:

  • Profile → Report a concern — opens email to our Child Safety contact with room details
  • Profile → Delete account — for account-level issues
  • Google Play listing — support contact for general feedback

When reporting CSAE or child safety issues, include the room code, display names involved, and what happened.

3. Action on CSAM and CSAE

When FoodRush obtains actual knowledge of CSAM or CSAE content, we take appropriate action without delay, including:

  • Removing the reported content where technically possible
  • Terminating the account(s) responsible
  • Closing or cancelling affected rooms
  • Preserving information as required for lawful reporting
  • Reporting confirmed CSAM to authorities as described below

We do not ignore reports. All CSAE and CSAM reports are treated as priority incidents.

4. Compliance with child safety laws

FoodRush complies with applicable child safety laws and regulations. When we confirm CSAM, we report it to:

  • The National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) via CyberTipline, where applicable, and/or
  • The relevant regional or national law-enforcement authority in the jurisdiction involved

If a child is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. You may also report abuse on Google products via Google’s reporting resources.
